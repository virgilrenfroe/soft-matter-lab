Crest travels · float stays

Specimen 01 — Wave basin

The crest travels. The water stays.

Note — energy crosses the tank. The colored floats do not.

A traveling wave carries energy from the orange paddle to the far wall.

The water under a crest mostly moves up and down.

Each float is a bit of material on a fixed mast. A float near the paddle crests before a float downstream.

A stone makes a ring. The ring spreads. The floats still stay on their masts.

Try this

Watch the gold float, then the blue one. Which crests first? Click the water to drop a stone. The ring moves. The float does not slide along its mast.

Neighbors share the pull

Specimen 02 — Cloth

The sheet shares the tension.

Note — pull one stitch. The threads around it take the load.

The cloth is a grid of points tied to their neighbors.

A force at one point becomes a curve across the sheet. That shared pull is tension.

The colored stitches are material. They stay in the weave. They move because the weave pulls them.

Wind is a push spread over the whole cloth. The sheet bows together.

Try this

Drag the lower cloth. Watch the tug climb toward the rod. Then raise wind, or hit Gust. The colored stitches move as one piece, not one thread at a time.

Ink is carried

Specimen 03 — Flow

Here the material moves.

Note — unlike the floats, each drop travels with the current.

In the wave tank, the floats stayed on their masts.

Here the ink is the material, and the current takes it downstream.

The clay post splits the stream. Lanes go around and meet again.

Slow the speed to zero and the ink stops. A wave crest would not.

Try this

Follow one large drop from the left, around the post, and off the right. Add swirl. Then set speed to zero and scroll back to the basin. What moved there? What moves here?

Specimen 04 — Centerpiece

Same question. Two soft bodies.

Note — left, the crest moves. Right, the load spreads.

Left: a crest runs the basin. The floats bob in place.

Right: wind loads the cloth. Neighbors share it.

Neither body is rigid. Both keep their material connected.

Soft matter is this: the shape changes, and the piece stays one piece.

Try this

Watch for ten seconds without touching. Name what travels on the left, and what stretches on the right. Then orbit either view.

For teachers

Short objectives, one classroom loop, and how the lab maps to shop practice and intro physics.

Learning objectives

  • Say that a traveling wave moves energy while the medium oscillates in place.
  • Point to a float and show that it does not travel with the crest.
  • Explain tension as a pull shared by neighboring parts of a cloth.
  • Contrast flow, which carries material, with a wave, which does not.

10-minute classroom activity

1) Specimen 01: pick one float. Does it cross the tank? 2) Drop a stone and sketch the ring. 3) Specimen 02: drag once and name what the neighbors do. 4) Specimen 03: follow one ink drop. Write one sentence on how flow differs from the wave.

Shop & continuum map

Ripple tanks, flags and shade cloth, wind on a loose cover, ink in a flume. Useful before anyone says “partial differential equation.”

Intro physics map

Transverse waves, wavelength and tempo, damping, tension in a membrane, advection versus wave motion.
